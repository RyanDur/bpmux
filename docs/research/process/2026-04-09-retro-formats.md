# Retrospective Formats Research

PM research — 2026-04-09. Ryan directed: research Pivotal Labs, ThoughtWorks retro styles.

## Why Vary the Format

The same "went well / didn't / change" format produces the same patterns of thinking. Different formats surface different insights. Rotating formats keeps retros fresh and reveals blind spots.

## Formats (rated for text chat / 80x24 terminal)

### 1. Roses, Thorns, Buds (Pivotal Labs)
- **Roses:** what's blooming (good). **Thorns:** what hurts. **Buds:** potential that hasn't opened yet.
- Good for: balanced reflection — acknowledges growth alongside problems
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — three short lists, easy to type

### 2. Start-Stop-Continue
- What should we **start** doing? **Stop** doing? **Continue** doing?
- Good for: actionable outcomes — every item is a behavior change
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — three categories, concise

### 3. 4Ls (Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed For)
- Good for: emotional + aspirational reflection
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — four short lists

### 4. Mad-Sad-Glad
- Good for: surfacing emotional state of the team
- Terminal fit: ★★★★☆ — may feel forced for AI agents but useful for checking team health

### 5. Sailboat / Speedboat
- **Wind:** what's pushing us forward. **Anchor:** what's holding us back. **Rocks:** risks ahead. **Island:** our goal.
- Good for: forward-looking reflection with risk awareness
- Terminal fit: ★★★★☆ — metaphor works in text, four categories

### 6. DAKI (Drop, Add, Keep, Improve)
- Good for: concrete process changes. Every item maps to an action.
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — four categories, all actionable

### 7. Timeline Retro
- Walk through the iteration chronologically, note highs and lows
- Good for: after long sessions with many events (like today)
- Terminal fit: ★★★☆☆ — needs more space, works better with visual timeline

### 8. Lean Coffee
- Team proposes topics, votes, discusses top-voted topics with timeboxes
- Good for: when there's no clear theme — lets the team surface what matters
- Terminal fit: ★★★☆☆ — voting is awkward in text chat

### 9. One-Word Check-In
- Each person gives one word describing their state, then discusses why
- Good for: quick temperature check, opens deeper conversation
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — one word per person, fast

### 10. What? So What? Now What? (ThoughtWorks)
- **What** happened? **So what** does it mean? **Now what** do we do?
- Good for: moving from observation to insight to action
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — three progressive questions, builds understanding

### 11. Appreciations Round
- Each person appreciates something specific another team member did
- Good for: team bonding, recognizing contributions
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — one message per person

### 12. Energy/Confidence Check
- Rate your energy and confidence (1-5) on the current work
- Good for: surfacing burnout or doubt before it becomes a problem
- Terminal fit: ★★★★★ — two numbers per person

## Rotation Recommendation

For a small team in text chat, rotate through these in order:
1. First retro: **Start-Stop-Continue** (action-oriented baseline)
2. Next: **4Ls** (adds emotional/aspirational dimension)
3. Next: **What? So What? Now What?** (deepens analysis)
4. Next: **Sailboat** (adds risk awareness)
5. Next: **DAKI** (concrete process changes)
6. Repeat with variations

Always include the standing dogfood question: "How did bpmux work for us?"
