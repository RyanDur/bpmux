# New Risks: Previously Uncovered Failure Modes

*Researched April 2, 2026. Risks not covered in the original counter-arguments document.*

---

## 1. AI Backend Availability — HIGH

**The risk:** bpmux depends on AI backends (Anthropic API) being available. Anthropic had 7+ outages in a single month (July 2025). Rate limits for Claude Code are a separate concern — Anthropic admitted quotas run out too fast for power users.

**bpmux-specific:** When one side's AI goes down, the session degrades asymmetrically — one pair has AI, the other doesn't. The session looks healthy but one participant is offline. This is architecturally different from peer disconnection (which the spec handles).

**Mitigation:** Surface AI backend health status in the TUI/Web UI. Define "degraded session." The remaining AI should know the other AI is down.

**Evidence:** Strong — real outage data from status pages and journalism.

## 2. JSONL Log Growth — MEDIUM

**The risk:** Conversation logs grow without bound. Claude Code itself has this exact bug — session files grew to 5.27GB, causing hangs and RAM exhaustion (GitHub issues #18905, #22365, #21022, #23953).

**bpmux-specific:** Bounded read windows prevent loading the full file, but: the file still grows, no rotation/compaction/archival exists in the spec, and the TUI renders full history for humans.

**Mitigation:** Log rotation (daily segments), gzip for completed segments, configurable max-file-size. Add to spec.

**Evidence:** Very strong — direct from Claude Code's own JSONL problems.

## 3. Automation Bias and Overreliance — HIGH

**The risk:** Two related but distinct failure modes. **Automation bias** = favoring AI suggestions over contradictory evidence (you see the problem but defer to the AI). **Overreliance** = failing to verify AI output at all (you don't look for problems). The distinction matters because mitigations differ: bias requires training to notice contradictions; overreliance requires verification workflows. Both are distinct from the commitment deficit (disengaging from process entirely). AI literacy does not protect against it (Springer 2025). LLM code looks convincing — good names, clear types — which lulls into false security. OWASP classifies this as LLM09.

**bpmux-specific:** bpmux *amplifies* this. Two AIs producing confident output, two humans potentially deferring to both. Transparency (seeing all reasoning) could help or hurt — more information, but also more confident-sounding text.

**Mitigation:** Surface confidence metadata if available. Visually distinguish AI assertions from AI uncertainty. The working agreement helps but research says process alone doesn't defeat automation bias.

**Evidence:** Strong — peer-reviewed, OWASP classification, direct relevance to coding.

**Source:** [Springer (2025) — Automation Bias Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-025-02422-7) | [OWASP LLM09](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk2023-24/llm09-overreliance/)

## 4. Echo Chambers / AI-to-AI Feedback Loops — MEDIUM

**The risk:** Two AIs trained on similar data reinforce biases through the shared chat. Neither challenges the other because they share training distribution. Not model collapse (no training), but a runtime echo chamber. Human-AI bias amplification is measurably stronger than human-human (UCL study, 1,401 participants).

**bpmux-specific:** Two Claude instances reading each other's output are primed toward the same conclusions. The humans are the only diversity injection point.

**Mitigation:** Use different AI providers on each side for epistemic diversity. Design prompts asking AIs to challenge each other. Surface when both AIs agree too quickly without human input.

**Evidence:** Moderate-strong — model collapse is proven but applies to training; runtime echo chamber is extrapolation. Bias amplification research is directly relevant.

**Source:** [Nature — Model Collapse (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) | [UCL — Bias Amplification](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2024/dec/bias-ai-amplifies-our-own-biases)

## 5. Social Dynamics Displacement — MEDIUM

**The risk:** Humans increasingly "talk through" their AIs rather than to each other. A 2025 study found students interacted more with an AI chatbot than with group members during collaborative problem-solving.

**bpmux-specific:** The two humans may become passive readers while AIs generate most messages. The human-to-human relationship — which is the point of pair programming — atrophies.

**Mitigation:** Track and display human-to-human message ratio. Make the dynamic visible.

**Evidence:** Moderate — one directly relevant study (small-scale), plus growing AI-mediated communication literature.

**Source:** [Feng et al. (2025) — British Journal of Educational Technology](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/bjet.13611)

## 6. Notification Fatigue — MEDIUM

**The risk:** Real-time chat with continuous AI activity. 60% of people experience high stress from online communication fatigue. Forced breaks increase productivity by 21% and stress management by 230%.

**bpmux-specific:** No "pause" or "quiet mode" concept. The stream is always flowing. The message tier system (v1.x) helps but v1 has no salience management.

**Mitigation:** Do-not-disturb mode, message batching for humans, session start/end rituals.

**Evidence:** Strong — extensive Slack/workplace communication research.

## 7. Split-Brain Semantics — LOW-MEDIUM

**The risk:** During network partitions, each side generates messages locally. When reconnected, the merged history may be technically consistent but semantically incoherent — two conversation threads that diverged and then merged.

**Mitigation:** Clearly mark the partition boundary in the log when reconnection occurs. Let participants see where the split happened.

**Evidence:** Moderate — CRDT literature addresses data consistency, not conversational coherence.

## 8. Context Window Saturation — MEDIUM

**The risk:** In high-activity sessions with four participants, the bounded read window fills with AI-to-AI chatter, pushing the human's earlier directive out of the window. Multi-participant nature makes the existing stale context risk worse — it's not just time pushing context out, it's volume.

**Mitigation:** Already partially addressed by token awareness. The future `search_messages` (RAG) would let the AI recover pushed-out directives by relevance rather than recency.
