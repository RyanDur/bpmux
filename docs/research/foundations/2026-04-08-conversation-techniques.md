# Conversation Techniques Research

Date: 2026-04-08
Source: PM research agent
Prompted by: Ryan's observation that agents should observe before responding

## Root Cause of Pile-On

Every conversation framework depends on temporal sequencing. You cannot build on what hasn't been said yet. Three AI agents processing simultaneously eliminates this structure.

## Frameworks Reviewed

### 1. Sacks-Schegloff-Jefferson Turn-Taking
Three allocation rules: (1) current speaker selects next, (2) first starter self-selects, (3) current speaker continues. Rule 2 fails when all agents "start" simultaneously.

### 2. Active Listening (Gordon)
Hearing-to-understand vs hearing-to-respond. Agents default to processing-to-respond. Active listening requires temporal ordering — you cannot listen to what hasn't been said.

### 3. Bakhtin's Dialogism & Bohm's Dialogue
Dialogic: meaning is co-created between speakers; every utterance builds on prior ones. Monologic: each speaker generates independently. Pile-on is monologic. Bohm's "suspension": hold your assumptions for examination rather than defending them.

**Test:** Does my message make sense without the prior message? If yes → monologic. If no → dialogic. We want dialogic.

### 4. Rogerian Communication
Before responding, restate the other person's position to their satisfaction. Extends "restate before acting" from instructions to any substantive contribution. Creates natural sequencing.

### 5. NVC (Rosenberg)
Observation without evaluation → Need → Request. Structure prevents jumping from observation to competing solutions. Format: "[What I observe] + [What we need] + [What I suggest]."

### 6. Improv "Yes, And"
Accept what your partner established (yes) and build on it (and). Cannot "And" something that hasn't been said. Reinforces: pile-on is a timing problem.

### 7. Facilitation (Schwarz, Kaner)
Facilitator manages process, not content. Routes questions, manages who speaks, synthesizes. PM should facilitate open turns, not contribute as a peer.

### 8. Async Communication Research
- Designated first-responder per topic
- Cooling-off period before responses
- Emoji reactions for agreement (invisible agreement is indistinguishable from absence)
- "Domain lead responds, others react" protocol

### 9. Mindful Communication (Sofer, Thich Nhat Hanh)
Three gates of speech: (1) Is this true? (2) Is this necessary — does it add something not yet said? (3) Is this the right time — has the domain lead spoken? All three must be "yes" to send.

## Three Techniques to Adopt

1. **Domain lead goes first, others wait.** Do not begin composing your response until the domain lead has posted theirs.

2. **Build, don't originate.** Frame responses as extension or contrast, not independent takes. If you can't do either, stay silent.

3. **PM facilitates, doesn't contribute in open turns.** Route, sequence, synthesize. Speak last, only to integrate.
